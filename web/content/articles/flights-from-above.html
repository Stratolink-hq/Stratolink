The dashboard draws each flight as a line on a map. That tells us where a balloon went, but not much about what it flew through. Satellite data gives a more tangible view.

GOES-18, one of NOAA’s weather satellites, sits about 36,000 km above the equator and takes a picture of the western US every few minutes. Laying a balloon’s track over those pictures shows the clouds below it, when it was flying in the dark, and where it went quiet. Press play, or drag along the timeline under each map, to move through a flight.

Stratolink 1

Stratolink 1 launched from San Francisco on October 23. It climbed out of the marine layer and drifted about 200 km down the coast before going silent off Big Sur.

Stratolink 3

Stratolink 3 took the same route in May and kept going: down past Baja, silent for 35 hours through a day and two nights, then heard again over Sonora on its way to New Mexico. The Baja Run follows the rest of its flight.

Over Spain

Eight days after its last packet over New Mexico, having crossed the Atlantic unheard, Stratolink 3 was picked up over western Spain by receivers there. Its GPS stuck again for most of the day, and it went quiet that night before turning up near Málaga the next evening.
