# UV replacement decision for the three-board v1.1 pilot

2026-10-13. **Separate UVA and UVB measurements would materially improve the ozone experiment. AS7331 is the strongest compact integrated candidate identified, including after the expanded direct-LCSC search.** It requires a local PCB and firmware change. LCSC does list multiband UV parts, but the inspected AS7331 entries are unavailable; catalog existence and assembly inventory are different facts. An available detector with one combined UV output does not provide the missing second measurement.

My recommendation is **pursue AS7331 for the multiband pilot, with supplier allocation and a local ECO resolved before assembly release**. The observed DigiKey bare-chip arrival estimate is November 9, not a guaranteed delivery. No UV population change, DNP choice, or order has been made by this review. A successful electrical prototype does not require completed ozone-retrieval validation before fabrication; it does require raw data, characterized optics and honest experimental labeling. If obtaining the assembled boards immediately is the overriding requirement, the UV population/sourcing trade-off needs an explicit decision rather than silently omitting the sensor.

## Why the other integrated parts are not equivalent

**ZOPT2202** measures separate UVA/UVB with compensation channels. Its 1.7–3.6 V, −40…90 °C, 2.0 × 2.2 × 0.7 mm LGA6 is attractive electrically and mechanically, but it is obsolete and its pins differ from U6: 1 SCL, 2 SDA, 3 VDD, 4 INT, 5 GND, 6 NC. Its raw mode requires a specific initialization and application compensation; the datasheet calls for another application note for digital trimming. It would need a new footprint, wiring and driver, not just a BOM substitution. [Renesas product status](https://www.renesas.com/en/products/zopt2202), [datasheet §§3.3–3.4](https://www.renesas.com/en/document/dst/zopt2202-datasheet).

**VEML6075** has separate outputs, but the primary datasheet puts the nominal “UVB” peak at 330 nm, in UVA, with a short-wavelength tail. A channel label does not establish an optimal ozone-sensitive UVB response. Its 4-pad 2 × 1.25 mm land pattern also differs from U6. [Vishay datasheet](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/1256/VEML6075.pdf).

**Si1133** actually measures UV, unlike a visible/IR-only UV-index estimate. Its ADC mux exposes UV and UV-Deep selections, but the inspected datasheet specifies one UV sensitivity point at 310 nm, not a characterized independent UVA/UVB pair; its dual ADCs also serve dark-reference cancellation. Establishing usable independent band responses would need spectral characterization rather than assuming “Deep” means a second desired band. The manufacturer-authored Rev 0.91 is marked Not Recommended for New Design. [Si1133 datasheet §§3.1–3.2, 7.2.1, 8](https://www.mouser.com/datasheet/2/368/Si1133-1397754.pdf).

**sGlux filtered UVA and UVB photodiodes** are a legitimate alternative family, but they are separate analog detectors requiring front-end circuitry and optical qualification. **Hamamatsu broad UV-enhanced photodiodes** need suitable filters and strong visible rejection to form a ratio instrument. Neither yields a verified in-stock, same-footprint LCSC digital substitute from this search. [sGlux primary catalog](https://sglux.de/en/product-category/sic-uv-photodiodes/).

## AS7331 integration scope

The native audit was rerun after the R4/U2 metadata promotion on canonical `hardware/pcb/stratolink.kicad_pcb`, SHA-256 `8e2f83797dd68195aa71b73fc252fe57e53ccd3683ef5f74739dc4d25d6ce8d5`. U6 is front-side at (69.098, 40.606) mm. Its six pins are +3.3 V, NC, GND, SCL, grounded INT, SDA, and still match the freshly exported schematic netlist. **The release has already corrected the old schematic/footprint identity mismatch.** The unused older `hardware/v1.1` tree is not the current release. [Promotion evidence](../sourcing_followup/promotion.json).

AS7331 uses a 2.60 × 3.65 × 1.09 mm OLGA16 body, 2.7–3.6 V supplies and I²C. At 3.3 V its specified active current is 1.42 mA typical / 2 mA maximum, standby 970 µA maximum, power-down 1 µA maximum at the electrical table's conditions. It needs a 3.3 MΩ ±1%, ≤50 ppm/K reference and local supply decoupling. The newly opened manufacturer-hosted PDF is the same v4-00 revision as the locally cached copy. [Manufacturer v4-00 datasheet](https://look.ams-osram.com/m/1856fd2c69c35605/original/AS7331-Spectral-UVA-B-C-Sensor.pdf).

Proposed net mapping for a future ECO:

| AS7331 pins | Proposed connection |
|---|---|
| 1, 2, 5, 6, 15, 16 VSSA; 11 VSSD | Ground; implement short analog/digital return connection near the chip |
| 3 VDDA; 10 VDDD | Existing 3.3 V, locally decoupled |
| 4 REXT | Specified reference to VSSA, same PCB side |
| 7 A1; 14 A0 | GND, selecting 0x74; existing MS5611 uses 0x76 |
| 12 SDA; 13 SCL | Existing I²C bus; verify combined pull-ups and rise time |
| 8 SYN; 9 READY | Optional test provisions; polled command mode needs no extra MCU pins. Give unused SYN a defined level; do not ground the READY output. |

The replacement needs all-new lands and escapes; the present six pads cannot be reused. The reviewed U7 routing immediately north of U6 includes a specific 0.128 mm passage next to U6 pad 6. Reopening this area requires a fresh U7 keepout, clearance, decoupling, placement, paste and optical-obstruction review; remove obsolete U6-specific rule exceptions if their geometry disappears. No replacement placement, routing or mass measurement has been done here. [Current local assembly review](../identities/README.md), [native audit](audit.json).

The modest measurement power is not the main obstacle. Calculated module-only costs are 6.6 mJ for one second at the table maximum, 110 µW measurement average at one second/minute, or 1.406 J for five minutes at typical active current. These exclude MCU, converter and bus overhead and do not select an exposure. Use explicit power-down after daylight windows; standby would consume 3.20 mW. Verify leakage and restart at the actual component temperature. A −40 °C rating does not establish operation in colder air or at low pressure.

Firmware requires a new driver and explicit hardware population selection, not an address change. Preserve bounded I²C timeouts, reset recovery, conversion-complete checks and the existing optical quiescence fault policy. AS7331 command mode can wake for one conversion and return to power-down. Log all raw channels, gain, integration time, divider, flags and die temperature with fresh UTC/position. Its shared exposure settings mean bright UVA and weak UVB may require bracketed exposures; retain simultaneous samples within each exposure.

The current telemetry only carries integer UVI and visible lux. **AS7331 does not replace visible ALS.** Mark legacy `ambient_lux` unavailable; do not manufacture lux from UV or leave stale values. Version the science payload and decoder to retain multiband counts and configuration. Search found no `ambient_lux` input to current firmware power decisions; power gating uses the ADC policy. The present LTR driver already treats a never-enabled absent sensor as quiescent, which supports a deliberate DNP build, but pilot firmware should explicitly select that population to avoid pointless retries. Sources: `firmware/src/sensor_ltr390.cpp`, `main.cpp`, `telemetry.cpp`, `include/telemetry.h`, `stratolink_pins.h`; hashes in the audit.

## What this adds to ozone measurement

A second UV band adds ozone-sensitive information. In a simplified calibrated direct-beam model,

`ln(B/A) = known spectral baseline − m × ozone_column × (effective_absorption_B − effective_absorption_A) + differential_nuisance`.

The ratio can cancel a common illumination factor. If differential cloud/aerosol transmission, angular filter shift, stray light or relative gain are unknown, ozone and those effects still cannot be separated from that ratio alone. Broad-band effective absorption also depends on the transmitted spectrum. This is a structural limitation, not something more ADC bits removes. A third UVC channel can be valuable diagnostically but may have very little solar signal below most of the ozone layer.

**Newly verified optical detail:** ams' design guide explicitly recommends detector incidence within ±10° because oblique rays shift/deform its interference filters. It places a diffuser **in front of angle-limiting optics**, not directly on the detector. The guide also confirms that gain/time settings are shared across channels and calls for dark, gain, temperature and spectral-response corrections. This resolves the older report's uncertainty about the incidence specification. [AS7331 Design Guidelines, §§2, 4, 7](https://look.ams-osram.com/m/45d5dfb4a22ecb04/original/AS7331_Design_Guidelines_PD001048_2-00.pdf).

An ozone prototype therefore needs an unobstructed, characterized optical collector; dark/linearity/saturation checks; angular and temperature sweeps; and a spectral forward model fitted and evaluated on independent data. Start fixed on the ground against a calibrated spectral reference, then add the real enclosure and motion. Published spectral double-ratio methods support that validation path, not a promised accuracy for this chip. [Egli et al., 2023](https://amt.copernicus.org/articles/16/2889/2023/).

The attainable product is initially raw/calibrated UV measurements and an experimental **ozone column above the balloon**. It is not local ozone concentration or automatically a vertical profile. The historical LTR390 note's numerical precision promises remain unsupported. Nothing here establishes a percentage ozone accuracy.

## Stocked analog alternative: useful, but a larger experiment

The practical discrete candidate is **GUVA-C22SD + GUVB-C21SD**, costing **$8.77 in detectors** at the observed single-unit prices, each in a 2.4 × 1.8 mm package. GUVA-C22SD responds over 240–370 nm; the DigiKey-linked 2018 GUVB-C21SD sheet gives 240–320 nm. These are overlapping responses, not isolated rectangular bands. Both are specified only to −30 °C. A newer GUVB sheet changes the stated responsivity and lower band edge, so the purchased lot needs a controlled specification and calibration. [GUVA-C22SD source](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/genicom-co-ltd/GUVA-C22SD/9960950), [GUVB-C21SD source](https://www.digikey.com/en/products/detail/genicom-co-ltd/GUVB-C21SD/10474936), [detailed comparison](discrete-lcsc.md).

Each produces photocurrent, so add two low-leakage transimpedance paths, feedback/compensation components and ADC acquisition, or an I²C ADC on a reference daughterboard. Two LCSC-stocked TSU111ICT amplifiers add **$3.662**, bringing detectors/amplifiers to **$12.432** before passives, ADC, optics and assembly. Their calculated amplifier-only idle consumption is **5.94 µW typical**, or **9.77 µW maximum at the specified full-temperature conditions**, at 3.3 V. ADC/reference power and actual PCB leakage remain to be designed. This supports a concrete ground experiment without promising a ready replacement for U6. [AFE sources and reproducible model](discrete-lcsc.md).

The older **GUVA-S12SD + GUVB-S11SD** comparison costs **$11.23 for detectors alone** and uses larger 3.5 × 2.8 mm packages. It has similar overlapping broad UV responses and the same −30 °C operating limit. Its higher price is for a different package pair, not a second quote for the smaller candidate. [GUVA-S12SD datasheet](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/6128/GUVA-S12SD%20%282%29.pdf), [GUVB-S11SD datasheet](https://mm.digikey.com/Volume0/opasdata/d220001/medias/docus/5786/GUVB-S11SD_Rev1.0_2018.pdf).

For scale only, GUVB's typical sunlight example gives 1.4 nA at UVI 1. A hypothetical 10 MΩ transimpedance gives 14 mV: about 17 counts on an ideal 12-bit, 3.3 V ADC. That illustrates why analog leakage, noise and offsets need attention; it is not a universal UVI conversion, selected amplifier design or ozone precision estimate. The detector itself needs no supply in photovoltaic mode. The amplifier budget above is only part of the complete acquisition budget.

ML8511A's one analog output is sensitive to both UVA and UVB together; replacing LTR390 with it would not produce a ratio. AS7341/AS7343/TCS3448 visible/NIR spectral parts similarly do not supply the needed short-UV channel. “Multispectral” alone is not the requirement. [ROHM ML8511A datasheet](https://www.rohm.com/documents/11303/4214223/FEDL8511A-02-e.pdf), [ams AS7343/TCS3448 product information](https://ams-osram.com/de/products/sensor-solutions/ambient-light-color-spectral-proximity-sensors/ams-as7343-spectral-sensor).
